Pathogenic _de novo_ missense variants in _KIF1A_ cause KIF1A-associated neurological disorder (KAND). Instead of bespoke, variant-specific therapies, this work supports a **polymorphism-targeted antisense oligonucleotide (ASO)** strategy. The ASO knocks down the mutant allele by targeting a common SNP on the same haplotype and spares the wild-type copy.

**My contributions**

- An automated workflow that uses **long-read sequencing** to infer _cis_ relationships between pathogenic _KIF1A_ variants and nearby common SNPs.
- An exhaustive cross-patient search that identified **four shared SNPs**. If targetable, these would allow **54 individuals** to receive allele-specific ASO therapy.
- Coverage modeling showing that targeting these shared sites could enable allele-specific intervention for **~89%** of patients. A 1000 Genomes–based greedy analysis indicates that **3 SNP sites** could reach ≈80% of individuals globally, and **6 sites** >90%.

Published in _Nature Communications_ (2026).
