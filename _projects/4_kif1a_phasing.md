- Applied a scalable strategy to selectively knock down _de novo_ missense _KIF1A_ while sparing wild-type, enabling a polymorphism-targeted antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) strategy instead of bespoke, variant-specific therapies.
- Developed an automated workflow using long-read sequencing data to infer _cis_ relationships between pathogenic _KIF1A_ variants and nearby common SNPs, and performed an exhaustive cross-patient search that identified four shared SNPs which, if targetable, would allow 54 individuals to receive allele-specific ASO therapy.
- Performed coverage modeling showing that targeting these shared SNP sites could enable allele-specific intervention for ~89% of patients, with a 1000 Genomes–based greedy analysis indicating that targeting 3 SNP sites could reach ≈80% of individuals globally and 6 sites >90%, supporting the feasibility of a limited ASO set targeting shared polymorphisms.

Published in [_Nature Communications_ (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67752-y).
