**The gap.** Esophageal atresia with or without tracheoesophageal fistula (EA/TEF) is a rare congenital anomaly of the foregut. Known risk genes explain only a minority of cases, and the contribution of rare _inherited_ variants has been poorly characterized.

**The approach.** We jointly analyzed _de novo_ and rare inherited variants from whole-genome and exome sequencing of **401 affected individuals**, using selection-coefficient estimates to model variant effect sizes.

**What we found**

- Both protein-truncating and missense variants contribute to EA/TEF risk, and their effect sizes rise with the strength of selection against them.
- _De novo_ coding variants are significantly enriched in **metabolic processes**. This points to new biological processes in human foregut development.
- A rare variant association analysis against **47,419 population controls** identified **five novel candidate risk genes** (FDR < 0.15), including _APC2_ (FDR < 0.05). These signals came mostly from rare inherited variants. That fits a complex architecture in which many genes confer large relative risk with moderate penetrance.
