Investigated the genetic architecture of esophageal atresia with or without tracheoesophageal fistula (EA/TEF), a rare congenital foregut anomaly, using whole-genome and exome sequencing of 401 affected individuals.

- Addressed the limited explanatory power of known risk genes and the poorly characterized role of inherited variants by jointly analyzing _de novo_ and rare inherited variants, stratified by selection coefficient estimates. Demonstrated that both protein-truncating and missense variants contribute to EA/TEF risk, with effect sizes positively correlated with selection strength.
- Identified significant enrichment of _de novo_ coding variants in metabolic pathways, implicating new biological processes in human foregut development.
- Proposed five novel candidate EA/TEF risk genes (FDR < 0.15) through rare variant association analysis against 47,419 population controls, including _APC2_ (FDR < 0.05). These associations were mostly driven by rare inherited variants, consistent with a complex architecture in which many genes contribute large relative risk with moderate penetrance.
