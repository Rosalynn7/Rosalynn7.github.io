I am a Staff Associate in the Shen Lab at Columbia University Irving Medical Center, where I work with Yufeng Shen and Wendy Chung. I study the genetic architecture of early-onset conditions: congenital anomalies such as esophageal atresia/tracheoesophageal fistula (EA/TEF) and congenital heart disease (CHD), neurodevelopmental disorders, and autism. My work combines population genetics, statistical genomics, and deep learning.

My research applies population genetic principles to infer the effects of genetic variants and their contribution to disease. Current projects:

  • Congenital anomalies. I jointly analyze de novo and rare inherited variants from whole-genome and exome sequencing of 401 individuals with esophageal atresia / tracheoesophageal fistula (EA/TEF). This revealed new candidate risk genes and implicated metabolic processes in human foregut development.
  • Noncoding variants. I estimate the fitness impact of noncoding variants from the human allele frequency spectrum, using RNA-binding protein binding and cross-species conservation as priors. As part of this, I’m building deep learning models of post-transcriptional regulation (ongoing).
  • Variant prioritization. I proposed selection coefficients as an inheritance-independent way to prioritize pathogenic variants, so that families without parental sequencing aren’t left behind.

I have also contributed to allele-specific ASO therapy for KIF1A-associated neurological disorder (Nature Communications, 2026) and to machine-learning variant ranking for rare disease (Genome Medicine, 2026). I help maintain Mutable, a public database of 79,000+ curated de novo variants.

I received my M.S. in Electrical Engineering from Columbia University and my B.S. in Electrical Engineering from Beijing Jiaotong University.

Research interests: genetic architecture of early-onset conditions · population genetics of human disease · rare and de novo variants · noncoding variant effects · deep learning for genomics
